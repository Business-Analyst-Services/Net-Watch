The pilot

Eight weeks, eight targets.

A pilot on the cameras Council already has. Proposed targets, to agree with Council before it starts.

Timeline

  1. Week 0Set upAgree targets, data agreement and security check. Council IT sets up a read-only export of camera images.
  2. Weeks 1–2Label and shadowStaff label 300 frames; the model is retrained and re-tested on unseen cameras. Net Watch runs alongside the usual daily check, with no emails.
  3. Weeks 3–6LiveMorning and urgent emails 7 days a week. The officer approves every job; the contractor closes jobs with a photo and the fill found.
  4. Week 7MeasureResults against the eight targets, plus officer and contractor feedback.
  5. Week 8DecideScale to all 37 traps, adjust, or stop. Report to Council.

Data agreement and retention

  • Council owns every image, label and record. The export is read-only; nothing is written back to SCADA.
  • Images kept 12 months for training, then deleted, except the labelled set kept for testing the model.
  • No personal data: frames show bushland, roads and people are blurred, resident reports carry no names.
  • Hosted in Australia (to confirm with Council IT).
  • No new sensors in the pilot. Any later sensor needs an owner, a maintenance plan and a retention rule first.
  • Switching off stops the emails. Council keeps the data, labels and model.

  • Planned trips, not wasted onesThe truck goes only to nets that need emptying, ranked ahead of the rain.
  • Cleaner streets and creeksA full net before a storm backs water up the pipe or tears off and sends about three-quarters of a tonne of litter downstream. Net Watch flags it first, weekends included.
  • Less hunting, more decidingInstead of scanning photos, the officer gets an automatic email of the nets that need action, and approves with one click.
