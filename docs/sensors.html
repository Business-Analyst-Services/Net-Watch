Sensing strategy

One model, five trap types, the cheapest sensor that answers the question

The camera is the primary sensor wherever there is a line of sight, because Council already owns 23 of them. Underground units have no view, so they get a level sensor instead. Everything else is a second signal for the hours the camera can't see (night, fog, lens water) or for damage the camera can't judge.

Principles behind the matrix

  • Vision first, hardware second. The CSIRO trial already proved 4G trail cameras work and LoRaWAN did not at these sites. Add hardware only where the camera is blind.
  • Two independent signals before "urgent". A blocked call that triggers a same-day truck should come from the camera and a physical sensor, or from the camera at high confidence twice.
  • The weighbridge ticket is a sensor. Mass removed per clean, already on the contractor invoice, calibrates every other reading against reality.
  • Night is "unusable", not "empty". IR frames are scored by a separate head or routed to the level sensor; a dark frame never counts as a clean trap.
  • Damage is its own state. A torn net scores as empty to a naive model. Load drop, tilt and torn-edge detection catch it and raise a repair job under Part G, not a clean under Part A.
  • Fleet economics. 13 pit baskets at $150 of hardware each is cheaper than one eductor visit, but not cheaper than a crew photo. Spend per trap in proportion to its cleaning cost.
