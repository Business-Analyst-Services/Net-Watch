End-of-pipe net or basket

A steel-cable net clamped to the end of a stormwater pipe, just before the creek. Most of Council's camera sites: Lackey St East and West, Bettington Rd, James Ruse Dr, Sturt St, Norwood Ave and others on the SCADA wall.

MVP focus. Paul Hackney, SME session: "If you wanted to target one type of system, concentrate on the nets."

How it fills

Rain washes litter down the pipe into the net. The net swells and sags as it fills. Nets are designed to detach from the pipe when full and hit by another surge, through a weak link, so the net doesn't tear the outlet apart. That is the whole problem: once a net is full, the next storm sends it and its contents into the creek. Council wants to intervene at 70–80% full.

What the evidence shows in each state

  • Empty: net hangs slack, pipe mouth visible through the mesh.
  • Filling: lower third swollen with leaves and packaging.
  • Clean due: net taut and rounded, sagging toward the ground.
  • Overdue: net at full stretch, pipe mouth hidden, material backing into the pipe.
  • Damaged / detached: mesh torn, or net pulled off the top of the pipe (Lackey St West, 13 Nov 2026; Paul's example in the SME session).
  • Can't see: leaves or a branch across the lens, fog, IR night frame.

Signal to read: Change in the net itself, not the scene. Paul's warning: dense leafy frames, and about 90% of days nothing changes. Score against the trap's own last clean frame, not a generic 'full' image.

When to email the officer

Email when fill ≥ 70% and the 12-hour forecast exceeds the rain this net has historically needed to fill the remainder; email at once for damaged or detached; email after 2 days of can't-see. Expected volume: one email a week or fortnight.
